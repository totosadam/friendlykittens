What is PKD, and why does it matter for Persians and Himalayans?
PKD (polycystic kidney disease) is an inherited condition that causes cysts in the kidneys and is a known risk in Persian-type breeds, including Himalayans. Our kittens are PKD negative, so there is no need to worry about that debilitating problem later.

What does a feline leukemia negative colony mean?
Feline leukemia virus (FeLV) is a contagious virus that weakens a cat’s immune system. Our colony is FeLV negative, and we keep it that way through a close, year-round working relationship with our vet.

Do you offer a health guarantee?

Common health questions about Himalayan and Persian cats

General guidance from our years raising Himalayans and Persians. It is not a substitute for your own vet’s advice.

What health problems are Himalayan and Persian cats prone to?
The best-known risks in Persian-type breeds are polycystic kidney disease (PKD), breathing and eye issues linked to the flat face, dental crowding, and skin or coat problems if the long coat is not groomed. Choosing a breeder who health-tests, works with a vet and lets you meet the parents lowers these risks. Our kittens are PKD negative, from a feline leukemia negative colony and guaranteed free of genetic defects.

How do I find a healthy Himalayan kitten?
Visit in person, meet the parents, and ask to see the health paperwork. Look for a PKD negative result, a vet bill of health, first shots, and a kitten that is bright, curious and used to being handled. Every Friendly Kittens kitten goes home with all of these.

What are the signs of a healthy kitten?
Clear, bright eyes; a clean nose and ears; a soft, clean coat with no bald patches; a clean bottom; a good appetite; and a kitten that is playful and curious rather than lethargic. Ask to see the vet paperwork as well.

What is brachycephalic syndrome in Persian cats?
Brachycephalic means flat-faced. In some Persians and Himalayans the short face can narrow the nostrils and airways, and affect the tear ducts. Signs to watch for are noisy breathing, breathing through the mouth, or heavy tearing. Talk to your vet if you notice any of these.

Why do Persian and Himalayan cats have watery eyes?
The flat face can make tears drain less easily, so some tearing and staining around the eyes is common. Wipe gently with a soft damp cloth each day. Thick, coloured discharge, squinting or a red eye needs a vet visit.

Can Himalayan cats have dental problems?
Yes. The shorter jaw can crowd the teeth, which can make plaque build up faster. Regular dental checks at your vet, and getting a kitten used to having its mouth handled, help keep teeth healthy.

Are Persian cats prone to kidney disease?
Persian-type breeds carry a known inherited risk of polycystic kidney disease (PKD), and older cats of any breed can develop kidney disease. That is why PKD-negative breeding matters. Our kittens are PKD negative.

Is PKD in cats curable?
No. PKD is inherited and cannot be cured, but it can be avoided through responsible breeding. Our kittens are PKD negative and guaranteed free of genetic defects.

What is FeLV, and can my kitten catch it?
Feline leukemia virus (FeLV) spreads between cats through close contact and weakens the immune system. Friendly Kittens is an FeLV negative colony. Keeping your cat indoors and asking your vet about FeLV vaccination are the best ways to protect it.

What is ringworm in kittens?
Ringworm is a fungal skin infection, not a worm. It causes round, scaly bald patches and can pass to people and other pets. Every Friendly Kittens kitten goes home with a vet bill of health confirming it is free of ringworm.

How do I stop hairballs in a long-haired cat?
Brush most days to remove loose hair before your cat swallows it. Your vet can also suggest a hairball-control food or remedy if they are frequent.

Why is my Himalayan kitten sneezing?
Occasional sneezing is often just dust or excitement. Repeated sneezing with runny eyes, nasal discharge, a poor appetite or low energy can mean an upper respiratory infection and should be seen by your vet. Call us too — we are always happy to help.

What should I do if my kitten has diarrhea after coming home?
A change of home and food can upset a kitten’s stomach, which is why we tell you exactly what they have been eating so you can keep it the same. If the diarrhea lasts more than a day, or your kitten is lethargic or not eating, call your vet.

How often should my Himalayan see the vet?
Kittens need visits for their booster shots and the rabies shot required by Virginia law. After that, adult cats usually have a yearly checkup. Senior cats often go twice a year.

What vaccines does a kitten need?
Kittens usually get a series of core vaccines, often called FVRCP, several weeks apart, plus a rabies vaccine, which Virginia law requires. Every Friendly Kittens kitten goes home with its first set of shots and the record in its adoption packet. Your vet will plan the rest.

When should I spay or neuter my kitten?
Most vets recommend spaying or neutering at around five to six months, but ask your own vet. We can offer spay and neuter at discounted rates through our vet.

Do Himalayan cats have eye problems?
Tear staining is the most common. Flat-faced breeds can also be more prone to eye irritation, so keep the fur around the eyes clean and see your vet if an eye looks red, cloudy or painful.

What happens if my kitten gets sick after adoption?
Call us right away, as well as your vet. Our kittens leave with a signed vet bill of health and a genetic defect guarantee, and our relationship with Blue Ridge Vets means we can usually help you work out what is going on quickly.

About Himalayan and Persian cats

What is a Himalayan cat?
A Himalayan is a Persian in build and coat with the colourpoint markings and blue eyes of a Siamese. Himalayans are calm, quietly affectionate and famously happy on a lap. They are our speciality.

What is the difference between a Himalayan and a Persian?
Both have the same long coat, broad face and gentle nature. The difference is colour: Himalayans are colourpoint with blue eyes, while Persians come in solid and bicolour coats. We raise both the same way, underfoot and handled daily.

What colours do your Himalayan kittens come in?
Our Himalayans come in seal, blue, flame, chocolate and lilac points. Our Persians come in solid and bicolour coats. Ask what colours are in the current litter.

Do Himalayan kittens change colour as they grow?
Yes. Colourpoint kittens are born pale, and the darker points on the face, ears, paws and tail fill in over the first months, so a young kitten will look lighter than the adult cat it becomes.

Are Himalayan or Persian cats hypoallergenic?
No cat is truly hypoallergenic, and long-haired breeds do shed. If anyone in your household has allergies, we recommend a visit to spend time with the kittens before you decide.

Are Himalayans a good first cat?
They can be. Himalayans and Persians are calm, gentle and happy indoors, which suits families, first-time owners and apartments. The one real commitment is regular brushing, and we will show you how we do it.

What is a Himalayan’s temperament like?
Calm, affectionate and unhurried. They like company and a lap more than climbing the curtains, and ours are raised around people so they are not shy or unfriendly.

What do seal, blue, flame, chocolate and lilac point mean?
They describe the colour of a Himalayan’s points, the darker face, ears, paws and tail. Seal is a deep brown, blue a soft grey, flame a warm orange-red, chocolate a lighter milk-chocolate brown and lilac a pale pinkish grey. All have blue eyes.

Is a Himalayan the same as a colourpoint Persian?
Essentially, yes. Some cat registries treat the Himalayan as a colourpoint variety of the Persian, so you will also hear “Himalayan Persian” or “colourpoint Persian”.

How big do Himalayan cats get?
Himalayans are medium to large, sturdy cats with a solid, cobby build. Most adults weigh somewhere around 7 to 12 pounds, with males usually larger than females.

How long do Himalayan and Persian cats live?
With good indoor care and regular vet visits, many live well into their teens.

Do Himalayan cats shed a lot?
Yes, the long coat sheds, especially with the seasons. Brushing most days catches the loose hair before it reaches your furniture and keeps mats from forming.

Do Himalayans like to be held?
Ours do. They are held, brushed and played with every day from the first week, so they grow up happy to be picked up and very happy on a lap.

Living with your kitten

How much grooming does a Himalayan need?
A long coat needs regular brushing, ideally a few minutes most days, to stay free of mats. Our kittens are brushed from the start, so they enjoy it rather than fight it, and we will show you how we do it.

Do Persians and Himalayans need their eyes cleaned?
Flat-faced breeds can get tear staining, so a gentle wipe around the eyes with a soft damp cloth is part of a good daily routine. We will show you what we do before your kitten goes home.

Are they good with children and other pets?
Yes. Ours are raised around people, kids and other animals, so they do very well as lap cats and with small children, and they are very compatible with other cats and pets.

Should Himalayans live indoors?
Yes. Long-coated breeds do best as indoor cats: the coat stays clean and mat-free, and they are safe from traffic, predators and disease.

What should I have ready for the first night?
A quiet room with litter, food, water and a bed, and time to sit on the floor with them. We will tell you exactly what they have been eating so you can keep it the same at first.

What should my kitten eat?
Keep to the food they have been eating here for the first while, then change gradually if you want to. We give feeding and grooming guidance for week one with every kitten.

Will you help after we take our kitten home?
Always. Call or email any time with a question about grooming, feeding or settling in. We stay a phone call away for the life of your cat.

How do I introduce a new kitten to my other cat?
Start the kitten in its own quiet room for a few days, let the cats smell each other under the door, then allow short supervised meetings. Our kittens are raised around other cats and pets, which helps them settle in quickly.

Does a Himalayan need a professional groomer?
Usually not. Regular brushing at home keeps the coat in good shape. Some owners also book an occasional trim or bath with a groomer, particularly in summer.

Can a Himalayan be left alone while I am at work?
Himalayans are calm and adapt well to a workday routine. If you are out for long hours, two kittens together keep each other company, and there is a multiple-kitten discount.

What supplies do I need for a new kitten?
A litter box and litter, food and water bowls, the same food they have been eating here, a bed, a scratching post, a few toys, a wide-tooth comb and a soft brush, and a sturdy carrier for the ride home.
